🛡️ Pension Deduction Rules

💡 Passive Pension Income is 100% Exempt:

CPP and EI premiums are never deducted from passive retirement income, including CPP benefits, OAS, GIS, or private/corporate pension payouts.

If You Choose to Work While Collecting:

CPP (Age 60–65):

Deductions from employment earnings are mandatory and build a Post-Retirement Benefit (PRB).

CPP (Age 65–70):

Voluntary. You can opt out of CPP deductions on earnings by filing CRA form CPT30.

Personal Savings & Wealth Assets

Your private retirement capital pool, including Tax-Free Savings Accounts (TFSA), non-registered brokerage funds, or real estate assets.

These elements provide maximum flexibility. They can be drawn down in early retirement (RA to 65) to postpone taking early CPP, or preserved as tax-free growth blocks to pass on to heirs.

Recommended Strategy: Deferring CPP to age 65 or 70 while drawing down on personal TFSAs first is often highly tax-efficient, especially considering the 2026 TFSA indexing room ($7,000).

Corporate Coordination

Defined Benefit (DB) pension schemes in BC coordinate closely with public programs. The Bridge Benefit and Temporary Annuity programs are designed to automatically end on the first of the month following your 65th birthday.
